This Sunday morning roundup covers local reports published after Saturday afternoon's edition. The outages and burglary below happened Saturday, Oct. 10. Outage figures are historical snapshots, not a statement of current service status. Times are Central.

## Alcorn: Glen and Corinth power outages reported Saturday

Wind from the remnants of Isaias disrupted service in parts of Alcorn County Saturday. [Alcorn County News reported](https://alcornnewsms.com/alcorn-county-news/isaias-power-outages-glen-corinth/) that Alcorn County Electric Power Association (ACE Power) identified a large outage in the Glen area and sent crews to assess damage. By **3:45 p.m.**, the co-op said it had located the cause and was making repairs. It also reported work on a pole behind Belk in Corinth that had interrupted service to nearby businesses.

The report says an ACE Power graphic showed **498 outages across its system at 2:30 p.m.**, while PowerOutage.us counted about **280 Alcorn County customers without power around 5 p.m.** Those counts describe different areas and times; they should not be added together or read as Sunday morning totals. The report did not confirm when every customer's service was restored. Residents should consult their electric provider for current outage information.

## Prentiss: Sheriff's office investigates stolen county truck

The Prentiss County Sheriff's Office is investigating a Saturday burglary at a county supervisor's district shed on **County Road 8311**, [Alcorn County News reported](https://alcornnewsms.com/local-news/prentiss-county-burglary-truck-stolen/) from the department's notice. Investigators said vehicle batteries and other items were taken, along with a **white 2007 Ford F-150 four-door crew cab** work truck. The investigation was active as of the Saturday evening report, which did not announce an arrest.

The sheriff's office asks anyone with information to call **662-728-6232** or contact Crime Stoppers, according to the report. Anyone who sees a matching truck should contact law enforcement rather than approach it.
