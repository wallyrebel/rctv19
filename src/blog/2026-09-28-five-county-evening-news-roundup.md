Monday afternoon brought a new fire danger message for the five-county area. Today's local reports also detail a Corinth student's academic recognition and a Baldwyn school career program. The Benton and Tippah burn bans discussed below were already in place; the Weather Service's Monday warning is the new update.

## Benton and Tippah counties: Fire danger elevated Monday

The [National Weather Service in Memphis warned Monday](https://www.weather.gov/meg/) that humidity could fall to 20–30% during the afternoon while grass and other fuels remain critically dry. Its graphic says grass fires could ignite and spread quickly through **7 p.m. Monday** and urges residents to obey local burn bans. The office also expects low humidity and elevated fire danger through Tuesday. This is a fire danger message, not a report of a new wildfire.

The existing **Benton and Tippah county burn bans remain in effect**, [Tippah News reported Monday](https://tippahnews.com/public-service/tippah-county-fire-danger-rain/). The [Mississippi Forestry Commission explains](https://www.mfc.ms.gov/burning-info/burn-bans/) that campfires, burn barrels and debris burning are prohibited during a ban; gas and charcoal grills are allowed when used safely. Residents should check current county restrictions before any outdoor burning. The bans and drought conditions were covered in earlier roundups, so today's development is the Weather Service's time-specific warning.

## Alcorn County: Corinth senior named National Merit semifinalist

Corinth High School senior **Thomas Senf** has been named a semifinalist in the 72nd National Merit Scholarship Program, [Alcorn County News reported Monday](https://alcornnewsms.com/alcorn-county-news/corinth-high-national-merit-semifinalist/), citing the Corinth School District. He is among about 16,000 semifinalists nationwide who can continue in the competition for scholarships. The report says Senf plans to study biology on a pre-medicine track.

## Prentiss County: Baldwyn students try skilled-trades technology

Baldwyn High School 10th graders used augmented and virtual reality equipment to explore skilled trades during a recent visit from Big Ideas, Inc., [Prentiss County News reported Monday](https://prentissnews.com/prentiss-county-news/baldwyn-high-students-explore-skilled-trades-through-ar-vr-experiences/). The report says the students also worked with carpentry learning equipment. The school visit happened before Monday's report; it is not described here as a same-day event.

No meaningful new Union County development was verified for this edition. Sunday's Riverfest and marching-band results have already been covered.
