<p>Dolly Mae Brock, 72, of Ripley, Mississippi, passed away peacefully on September 27, 2026, at Tippah County Hospital. She was born on July 13, 1954, in Arkansas.</p>

<p>Arrangements are incomplete at this time and will be posted as soon as they become available.</p>

<p>Dolly was a woman who could truly do a little bit of everything. She was a jack of all trades, always willing to work, learn, and take on whatever life brought her way. Her last job was selling pictures, a job she especially enjoyed because it gave her the opportunity to do what she loved most—socialize and spend time with people. Dolly never met a stranger and could carry on a conversation with just about anyone. Her friendly spirit and love for people were a special part of who she was.</p>

<p>Dolly also enjoyed fishing and treasured the time she spent with her family. Above all else, her family was the most important thing in her life. She loved her  entire family with her whole heart.</p>

<p>Later in life, Dolly proudly earned her GED, showing the determination and strength that carried her through life. Most importantly, Dolly was a woman of strong faith and was a member of Turners Chapel. She was sure of what she believed and was ready to meet her Lord and Savior. Her faith brought her comfort, and she looked forward to the day she would see her God.</p>

<p>Dolly is survived by her loving husband, James Brock; her son, Jerry (Stacy) Brock; grandchildren, Trenton (Payton) Brock, Randa Brock, Readus Brock, and Reagan Brock; and great-grandchild, Elena Brock.</p>

<p>She is also survived by her siblings, Johnny (Annette) Davis, Ellis (Debbie) Davis, Maggie (Darrell) Bridges, Maryann White, Paulette Davis, and Loretta Davis, along with a host of nieces and nephews whom she loved as her own. Dolly shared a special bond with them and cherished each one dearly.</p>

<p>She was preceded in death by her parents, Roy Davis and Hattie Willard Houge Davis; her son, Ronnie Brock; and her siblings, Eula Potts, Hollis Davis, Edward Davis, Billy Davis, Thomas Davis, Janie Hall, and Helen Davis.</p>

<p>Dolly leaves behind a lifetime of memories, laughter, conversations, and love. She will be deeply missed by all who knew and loved her, but her family finds comfort knowing that she was ready and that she is now at peace with her Lord.</p>
