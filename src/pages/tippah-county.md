Tippah County sits in the north Mississippi hill country, tucked against the Tennessee line about an hour southeast of Memphis. [Ripley](/communities/ripley/) is the county seat. This page collects the facts, offices and phone numbers residents and visitors look up most — and it is kept current by RCTV19, which has covered the county since 1999.

## Where the name comes from

**Tippah** comes from a Chickasaw word meaning "cut off," by way of the Tippah River that runs through the region. The county was organized in 1836, in the wave of county creation that followed the 1832 Treaty of Pontotoc Creek and the removal of the Chickasaw from north Mississippi.

## Geography and neighbors

Tippah County borders five counties across two states:

- **North** — Hardeman County, Tennessee
- **Northeast** — Alcorn County, Mississippi
- **Southeast** — Prentiss County, Mississippi
- **South** — Union County, Mississippi
- **West** — Benton County, Mississippi

Part of the **Holly Springs National Forest** lies within the county. **Mississippi Highway 15** is the county's spine, running north-south from Walnut through Ripley to Blue Mountain and on toward New Albany. **U.S. Highway 72** crosses the northern end at [Walnut](/communities/walnut/), carrying traffic between Memphis and Corinth.

**What each office handles.** The **Circuit Clerk** registers voters and handles elections and circuit court records. The **Chancery Clerk** keeps land records, deeds and county financial records. The **Tax Collector** collects property taxes and sells car tags — including taxes on manufactured homes, motorcycles, motor homes, trailers and airplanes, plus use tax on items brought into Mississippi and sales tax on private-party vehicle sales. The **Tax Assessor** sets the assessed values those taxes are figured on.

## Schools

Tippah County is served by two public school districts.

**North Tippah School District** — serving Walnut, Falkner, Chalybeate and Tiplersville. Central office: 20821 Highway 15, Falkner, MS 38629. Superintendent: Dr. Dax Glover.

- Walnut Attendance Center — the **Wildcats**
- Falkner Attendance Center — the **Eagles**
- Chalybeate Elementary School — grades K-8

**South Tippah School District** — serving Ripley, Blue Mountain, Dumas and Pine Grove. Central office: 402 Greenlee Street, Ripley, MS 38663. Phone: 662-837-7156.

- Ripley High School — the **Tigers**
- Ripley Middle School
- Ripley Elementary School
- Blue Mountain School — the **Cougars**
- Pine Grove School — the **Panthers**

**Higher education.** [Blue Mountain Christian University](/communities/blue-mountain/), founded in 1873, is a private liberal arts university supported by the Mississippi Baptist Convention. Its teams, the **Toppers**, compete in the NAIA's Southern States Athletic Conference.

## Health care

**Tippah County Hospital**, 1005 City Avenue North, Ripley — 662-837-9221. Emergency services are available 24 hours. In an emergency, call **911**.

**North Mississippi Primary Health Care** operates clinics in the area and, with the **Mid-South Food Bank**, runs periodic food pantry distributions in the county, frequently at the Walnut Fire Department, 27880 Highway 15. Distribution dates are announced on RCTV19 as they are scheduled.

## History worth knowing

**Colonel William Clark Falkner** dominates Ripley's 19th-century history: railroad builder, Confederate officer and the author of *The White Rose of Memphis*. He was killed on the Ripley square in 1889. His great-grandson, the novelist **William Faulkner**, modeled Colonel John Sartoris on him, and the town of [Falkner](/communities/falkner/) carries his name. Ripley's historic district is on the National Register of Historic Places.

The county also sits inside the **north Mississippi hill country blues** tradition, the raw, droning, one-chord style that is distinct from Delta blues. The annual Burnside Music Fest on the Ripley square is built around that heritage — see the [Burnside Music Fest page](/topics/burnside-music-fest/).

One of President Bill Clinton's great-grandfathers is buried in the county.
